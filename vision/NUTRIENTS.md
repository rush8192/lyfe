Nutrients are the key resources that organisms need to gather from their environment to sustain life, store energy, move, reproduce, and evolve. Nutrients include both macro elements that are abundant in all life (e.g. the building blocks of DNA, carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, and phosphorus, plus sulfur), as well as micro nutrients that are found in smaller quantities

Some nutrients are found in a biologically-available form in their environment; others need to be processed by organisms into a usable form (e.g. nitrogen and carbon fixation)

# Macronutrients

The 'big six', CHNOPS, make up 97% of all life:

## Carbon

Found in the air as CO2, carbon must be 'fixed' into a usable form by an organism's metabolism. Dying organisms may release fixed carbon back into the environment in organic compounds that may be consumed by organisms directly; predatory organisms may take a more direct route to acquiring these resources. Some carbon can be produced by geological or biological sources as methane gas (CH4).

## Hydrogen

Released from volcanic sources as H2 and H2S (fuel for early metabolisms), found as an inorganic compound in ammonia (NH3) and plentiful but hard to access directly in water (H20), or found from biological or natural sources as method (CH4).

## Oxygen

Although initially absent as free oxygen (O2), certain metabolisms that extract carbon from CO2 can start producing oxygen as a waste product (e.g. photosynthesis)

When free oxygen is present, it can be used by organisms that use respiration for their metabolism.

## Nitrogen

Atmospheric nitrogen (N2) is plentiful but inert, so other sources were required for early life. Some non-biological sources of available nitrogen exist as ammonia (NH3) or nitrates (NO3), but mass production of fixed nitrogren is only possible once organisms evolve that capability.

## Phosphorus

Bio-available Phosphorus was fairly rare in the early earth environment, but was produced gradually via the weathering of volcanic igneous rocks, as well as more sporadically from lightning strikes. Bio-available forms of phosphorus are commonly found as phosphates (PO4).

## Sulfur

Volcanic vents producing hydrogen sulfide (H2S) and sulfur dioxide (SO2) provided an important fuel for early metabolisms. Once oxygen became more plentiful, sulfur-fixing organisms became more niche.

# Micronutrients

While not as plentiful as the macronutrients, various other elements are used by different metabolisms, and are required for organisms to live and thrive:

- Calcium
- Iron
- Potassium
- Sodium
- Magnesium
- Zinc
- Copper
- Iodine
- Fluoride
- Selenium

# Nutrient decay

Some nutrients (especially gasses) will naturally decay, simulating radiation-induced chemical breakdown or atmospheric losses into space.
